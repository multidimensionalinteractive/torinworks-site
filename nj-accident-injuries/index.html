Top 5 Car Accident Injuries in New Jersey

Car accidents across New Jersey — from the Turnpike to local county roads — can cause serious, life-altering injuries. Understanding these injuries and your rights under New Jersey personal injury law is the first step toward protecting yourself and your family.

Understanding Your Injury

Most Serious Car Accident Injuries in NJ

If you or a loved one suffered any of these injuries in a New Jersey car accident, you may be entitled to compensation for medical bills, lost wages, pain and suffering, and long-term care. New Jersey's statute of limitations for personal injury claims is generally two years from the date of the accident — acting quickly matters.

1. Traumatic Brain Injuries (TBI)

Includes concussions up to severe brain damage. These injuries often cause lasting cognitive, emotional, and behavioral changes — sometimes permanently. Even mild TBIs can be problematic and still impactful. In New Jersey, TBI cases frequently result in significant settlements due to the need for ongoing rehabilitation, neuropsychological care, and potential loss of earning capacity. If you hit your head during a car accident in NJ, seek immediate medical evaluation even if symptoms seem minor.

2. Spinal Cord Injuries

Damage to the spine can result in partial or complete paralysis. Car accident injuries involving paraplegia or quadriplegia often lead to very large settlements due to lifelong care needs, lost earning capacity, and major lifestyle changes. New Jersey courts recognize the devastating impact of spinal injuries, and victims may recover damages for home modifications, wheelchairs, in-home care, and loss of consortium.

3. Severe Back and Neck Injuries

These car accident injuries include but are not limited to herniated discs or chronic whiplash. While more common, they can still be significant if they lead to chronic pain, surgery, or inability to work. Many New Jersey car accident victims underestimate whiplash — but cervical spine injuries can require months of physical therapy, injections, or even fusion surgery. Documenting these injuries early strengthens your claim.

4. Internal Organ Damage

Car accident injuries to organs like the liver, lungs, or kidneys can be life-threatening and require emergency treatment or surgery. These legal cases often involve high medical costs and serious risk. Because internal injuries may not show immediate symptoms, NJ emergency room protocols after serious collisions typically include CT scans and blood work. Delayed diagnosis can be fatal — always get checked after a major accident.

5. Broken Bones (Especially Complex Fractures)

Simple fractures from car accidents are less impactful legally, but compound fractures, multiple breaks, or injuries requiring surgery can lead to substantial claims — especially if they result in long-term impairment. In New Jersey, broken bone settlements factor in surgery costs, physical therapy, time off work, and whether the fracture heals with permanent limitations or arthritis.

New Jersey Law

NJ Personal Injury Basics

  • Statute of Limitations: You generally have 2 years from the date of the accident to file a personal injury lawsuit in New Jersey.
  • Comparative Negligence: New Jersey follows a modified comparative negligence rule. You can recover damages if you are less than 50% at fault, but your award is reduced by your percentage of fault.
  • No-Fault Insurance: New Jersey is a no-fault state. Your own Personal Injury Protection (PIP) covers initial medical expenses, but serious injuries may allow you to step outside no-fault and sue the at-fault driver.
  • Serious Injury Threshold: To sue for pain and suffering in NJ, your injury must meet the "serious injury" threshold — including permanent injury, significant disfigurement, or loss of a body part/function.
What to Do

After a Car Accident in New Jersey

  1. Seek medical attention immediately — even if you feel fine. Some injuries appear hours or days later.
  2. Report the accident to police and your insurance company within the required timeframes.
  3. Document everything — photos, witness contact info, medical records, and missed work.
  4. Consult a New Jersey personal injury attorney before accepting any insurance settlement.
  5. Do not post about the accident on social media — insurance companies monitor these posts.
